Connections that look right but are wrong

  • Deutsch–Jozsa is not an HSP. A balanced function need not be constant on the cosets of any subgroup. It only shares the 𝖧, Of, 𝖧 template.
  • Bernstein–Vazirani is not a standard HSP run. There H = ker f and H⊥ = {0, s}, so Fourier sampling would output 0 or s with probability 1/2 each. BV gets s deterministically in one query because f is itself a character and phase kickback prepares that character directly.
  • \(\mathsf{H}^{\otimes n}\) is not the QFT over \(\mathbb{Z}_{2^n}\). It is the QFT over \(\mathbb{Z}_2^n\). The cyclic group has carries between bits, which is why it needs controlled rotations.
  • Order finding is not an exact HSP over \(\mathbb{Z}_{2^n}\) unless r divides 2ⁿ. It is an HSP over ℤ with H = rℤ, sampled through a finite window, which is why continued fractions are needed.
  • Phase estimation and HSP: neither contains the other. Phase estimation handles arbitrary real phases; HSP handles arbitrary finite abelian groups. Order finding sits in both.
  • H ∩ H⊥ need not be {0}. In ℤ₂³ with s = 110, the element 110 is in both H and H⊥. What always holds: |H|·|H⊥| = |G| and (H⊥)⊥ = H.

L11–12 · Shor as phase estimation

Two views of Shor

Ua multiplies by a mod N, so on the orbit of 1 it is a cyclic shift. Its eigenphases are j/r. Feeding |1⟩ (a uniform mix of eigenvectors) into phase estimation outputs y ≈ jQ/r for a random j. Continued fractions turn y/Q into r; then the gcd step factors N.

Coset state to H⊥, from scratch

L13 · hub

Let \(G\) be finite abelian, \(H\le G\), and \(\mathrm{QFT}_G|x\rangle = \frac{1}{\sqrt{|G|}}\sum_{y\in G}\chi_y(x)|y\rangle\). Compute the probability of measuring \(y\) after applying \(\mathrm{QFT}_G\) to \(|g_0+H\rangle = \frac{1}{\sqrt{|H|}}\sum_{h\in H}|g_0+h\rangle\).

Hint 1 · setup

Write \(\langle y|\mathrm{QFT}_G|g_0+H\rangle\) with both normalizations. Use \(\chi_y(g_0+h)=\chi_y(g_0)\chi_y(h)\).

Hint 2 · key trick

If \(\chi_y(h')\ne1\) for some \(h'\in H\), re-index the sum by \(h\mapsto h+h'\). The sum is unchanged but also multiplied by \(\chi_y(h')\).

Hint 3 · finish

So the sum is \(|H|\) or \(0\). Square, then check the probabilities over \(H^\perp\) add to 1 using \(|H^\perp| = |G|/|H|\).

Prefactor in front of \(\sum_{h}\chi_y(h)\)?
Where does the offset \(g_0\) appear in \(\Pr[y]\)?
\[ \langle y|\mathrm{QFT}_G|g_0+H\rangle = \frac{\chi_y(g_0)}{\sqrt{|G||H|}}\sum_{h\in H}\chi_y(h) = \begin{cases}\chi_y(g_0)\sqrt{|H|/|G|} & y\in H^\perp\\ 0 & \text{otherwise}\end{cases} \]

So \(\Pr[y] = |H|/|G|\) on \(H^\perp\), uniform, independent of \(g_0\). Total \(|H^\perp|\cdot|H|/|G| = 1\). Measuring the output register first is optional: every coset gives the same distribution (no-signaling).

Why O(log |G|) samples are enough

L13

Let \(K\) be the subgroup generated by the samples so far. Show that while \(K\ne H^\perp\), each new sample has probability at least 1/2 of growing \(K\), and that \(K\) can grow at most \(\log_2|G|\) times.

Hint 1

\(K\) is a proper subgroup of \(H^\perp\). What does Lagrange's theorem say about \(|K|\)?

Hint 2

A uniform sample from \(H^\perp\) lands in \(K\) with probability \(|K|/|H^\perp|\le 1/2\).

Hint 3

When a sample lands outside \(K\), the new generated subgroup strictly contains \(K\), so its size is a multiple of \(|K|\) bigger than \(|K|\).

Do \(\log_2|G|\) samples always generate \(H^\perp\)?

\(|K|\) divides \(|H^\perp|\) and \(K\ne H^\perp\), so \(|K|\le|H^\perp|/2\). Each sample escapes \(K\) with probability \(\ge 1/2\), and each escape at least doubles \(|K|\). Since \(|H^\perp|\le|G|\), at most \(\log_2|G|\) escapes are needed; with \(O(\log|G|+\log(1/\delta))\) samples all of them happen with probability \(\ge 1-\delta\).

Contrast: cyclic ℤ_N needs only about 2 samples (H⊥ is cyclic, and two random multiples are coprime with probability ≈ 6/π²). Simon needs about n because H⊥ has rank n − 1.

Simon as an HSP

L8 → L13

\(G=\mathbb{Z}_2^3\), \(s=110\). List \(H\) and \(H^\perp\). Is \(H\cap H^\perp=\{0\}\)?

Hint 1

Characters of \(\mathbb{Z}_2^3\): \(\chi_y(x)=(-1)^{y\cdot x}\). So \(y\in H^\perp\iff y\cdot s\equiv 0\pmod 2\).

\(H\cap H^\perp\)?

\(H=\{000,110\}\), \(H^\perp=\{000,001,110,111\}\). \(|H|\cdot|H^\perp| = 2\cdot4 = 8 = |G|\). Here \(H\subset H^\perp\).

L13

\(g=3\) has order \(r=6\) mod 7, and \(h=4=3^s\). With \(f(a,b)=g^ah^b\) on \(\mathbb{Z}_6\times\mathbb{Z}_6\), find \(H\) and \(H^\perp\). Then find all \(s\) consistent with each sample set: (a) \((1,4)\); (b) \((2,2)\); (c) \((2,2)\) and \((3,0)\).

Hint 1

\(f(a,b)=g^{a+sb}\). It is constant exactly when \(a+sb\) is fixed mod 6.

Hint 2

\(H=\langle(-s,1)\rangle\). A character \((y_1,y_2)\) is trivial on it iff \(-sy_1+y_2\equiv0\pmod 6\).

The congruence \(y_2\equiv sy_1\) is mod
Do you need a sample with \(\gcd(y_1,6)=1\)?

\(H^\perp=\{(y_1,sy_1 \bmod 6)\}\). (a) \(s=4\). (b) \(2s\equiv2\pmod 6\Rightarrow s\in\{1,4\}\). (c) adding \(3s\equiv0\Rightarrow s\) even, so \(s=4\). Check: \(3^4=81\equiv4\pmod 7\).

Phase kickback, and when it entangles

L7 · L12

Apply controlled-\(U\) to \(|+\rangle|\psi\rangle\) where \(U|\psi\rangle=e^{i\theta}|\psi\rangle\). Then redo it with \(|\phi\rangle=\alpha|\psi_0\rangle+\beta|\psi_1\rangle\), two eigenvectors with different phases.

Hint 1

Expand by linearity and pull the eigenvalue out as a scalar.

With the superposition input, is the result a product state?

Eigenvector: \(\tfrac{1}{\sqrt2}(|0\rangle+e^{i\theta}|1\rangle)\otimes|\psi\rangle\), unentangled. Superposition: \(\tfrac{1}{\sqrt2}\sum_k c_k(|0\rangle+e^{i\theta_k}|1\rangle)|\psi_k\rangle\), entangled when the \(\theta_k\) differ. This is exactly why Shor's input \(|1\rangle\) gives a random \(j/r\).

Where \(8/\pi^2\) comes from

L12

Worst case \(\gamma=y_0+\tfrac12\). Get the limiting value of \(P(y_0)+P(y_1)\) from an integral, then state the exact finite-\(Q\) value.

Hint 1

\(\alpha_{y_0}=\frac1Q\sum_x e^{\pi i x/Q}\approx\int_0^1 e^{\pi i t}dt\).

Hint 2

For the exact value, sum the geometric series: \(\big|\sum_{x=0}^{Q-1}e^{i\phi x}\big| = \big|\sin(Q\phi/2)/\sin(\phi/2)\big|\).

At finite \(Q\), is each bin's probability above or below \(4/\pi^2\)?
\[ \int_0^1 e^{i\pi t}dt=\frac{2i}{\pi},\qquad \Big|\tfrac{2i}{\pi}\Big|^2=\frac4{\pi^2},\qquad P(y_0)+P(y_1)=\frac{2}{Q^2\sin^2\!\big(\frac{\pi}{2Q}\big)}\ \ge\ \frac8{\pi^2}. \]

Check in the Lab: microscope, "Worst case", n = 7 gives 0.8106.

Eigenvectors of \(U_7\) mod 15

L12

Find the orbit of 1 under multiplication by 7 mod 15, all eigenvectors \(|\psi_j\rangle\) with eigenvalues, and show \(|1\rangle=\frac12\sum_j|\psi_j\rangle\).

Hint 1

On the orbit, \(U_7\) is a cyclic shift. Shifts are diagonalized by Fourier vectors.

Orbit \(1\to7\to4\to13\to1\), \(r=4\), \(\omega=i\). \(|\psi_j\rangle=\tfrac12(|1\rangle+i^{-j}|7\rangle+i^{-2j}|4\rangle+i^{-3j}|13\rangle)\), \(U_7|\psi_j\rangle=i^j|\psi_j\rangle\). Summing over \(j\) kills every term but \(|1\rangle\).

QFT over \(\mathbb{Z}_8\) vs over \(\mathbb{Z}_2^3\)

L10 → L13

From \(\mathrm{QFT}_{\mathbb{Z}_8}|x\rangle=\frac1{\sqrt8}(|0\rangle+\omega^{4x}|1\rangle)(|0\rangle+\omega^{2x}|1\rangle)(|0\rangle+\omega^{x}|1\rangle)\), \(\omega=e^{2\pi i/8}\), derive the gates on each wire. Then say which gates disappear for \(\mathbb{Z}_2^3\) and why.

Hint 1

Write \(x=4x_1+2x_2+x_3\) and reduce each \(\omega^{kx}\) using \(\omega^8=1\).

Hint 2

A wire must still hold its bit while it controls a rotation, so its Hadamard comes after.

Does the H, CR₂, CR₃ / H, CR₂ / H circuit output the factors in the order written?

\(\omega^{4x}=(-1)^{x_3}\); \(\omega^{2x}=(-1)^{x_2}i^{x_3}\); \(\omega^x=(-1)^{x_1}i^{x_2}\omega^{x_3}\). Wire 1: H, CR₂ (control 2), CR₃ (control 3). Wire 2: H, CR₂ (control 3). Wire 3: H. Then reverse wires. \(n(n-1)/2\) rotations total.

For \(\mathbb{Z}_2^3\) the characters are \((-1)^{y\cdot x}\): no carries between bits, so every controlled rotation drops out and \(\mathrm{QFT}_{\mathbb{Z}_2^3}=\mathsf{H}^{\otimes3}\).
