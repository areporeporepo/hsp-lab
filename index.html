Connections that look right but are wrong

  • Deutsch–Jozsa is not an HSP. A balanced function need not be constant on the cosets of any subgroup. It only shares the \(\mathsf{H}^{\otimes n}, O_f, \mathsf{H}^{\otimes n}\) template.
  • Bernstein–Vazirani is not a standard HSP run. There \(H=\ker f\) and \(H^\perp=\{0,s\}\), so Fourier sampling would output \(0\) or \(s\) with probability \(\tfrac12\) each. BV gets \(s\) deterministically in one query because \(f\) is itself a character and phase kickback prepares that character directly.
  • \(\mathsf{H}^{\otimes n}\) is not the QFT over \(\mathbb{Z}_{2^n}\). It is the QFT over \(\mathbb{Z}_2^n\). The cyclic group has carries between bits, which is why it needs controlled rotations.
  • Order finding is not an exact HSP over \(\mathbb{Z}_{2^n}\) unless \(r \mid 2^n\). It is an HSP over \(\mathbb{Z}\) with \(H=r\mathbb{Z}\), sampled through a finite window, which is why continued fractions are needed.
  • Phase estimation and HSP: neither contains the other. Phase estimation handles arbitrary real phases; HSP handles arbitrary finite abelian groups. Order finding sits in both.
  • \(H\cap H^\perp\) need not be \(\{0\}\). In \(\mathbb{Z}_2^3\) with \(s=110\), the element \(110\) is in both \(H\) and \(H^\perp\). What always holds: \(|H|\cdot|H^\perp|=|G|\) and \((H^\perp)^\perp=H\).
  • “Non-abelian is hard because there is no QFT” is wrong. Efficient QFT circuits exist for \(S_n\) (Beals 1997) and \(D_N\). The bottleneck is turning the measurement “clues” into \(H\).
  • Measuring the output register does not cause the interference. Skip it and the first register is a mixture of coset states with the same output statistics.

L11–12 · Shor as phase estimation

Two views of Shor

\(U_a|y\rangle=|ay \bmod N\rangle\) is a cyclic shift on the orbit of \(1\), with eigenphases \(2\pi j/r\). Feeding \(|1\rangle=\frac{1}{\sqrt r}\sum_j|\psi_j\rangle\) into phase estimation outputs \(y\approx jQ/r\) for a uniformly random \(j\). Continued fractions turn \(y/Q\) into \(r\); then \(\gcd(a^{r/2}\pm1,N)\) factors \(N\).

L14 · Grover's algorithm

Two reflections make a rotation

Everything happens in the plane spanned by \(|w\rangle\) (uniform over the \(M\) marked items) and \(|r\rangle\) (uniform over the rest). The start state \(|s\rangle\) sits at angle \(\theta\) with \(\sin\theta=\sqrt{M/N}\). The oracle \(O\) reflects about \(|r\rangle\); the diffusion \(D=2|s\rangle\langle s|-I\) reflects about \(|s\rangle\). Apply them one at a time and watch the arrow and the bars.

L14 · Amplitude estimation

Phase estimation on the Grover iterate

In the \(|w\rangle,|r\rangle\) plane, \(G\) is a rotation by \(2\theta\), so its eigenvalues there are \(e^{\pm 2i\theta}\), and \(|s\rangle\) is an equal mix of the two eigenvectors. Phase estimation with \(m\) qubits reads \(\theta\), hence \(a=\sin^2\theta\), using \(2^m-1\) applications of \(G\). Compare with estimating \(a\) from \(2^m-1\) classical samples (the same number of oracle calls).

Coset state to \(H^\perp\), from scratch

L13 · hub

Let \(G\) be finite abelian, \(H\le G\), and \(\mathrm{QFT}_G|x\rangle = \frac{1}{\sqrt{|G|}}\sum_{y\in G}\chi_y(x)|y\rangle\). Compute the probability of measuring \(y\) after applying \(\mathrm{QFT}_G\) to \(|g_0+H\rangle = \frac{1}{\sqrt{|H|}}\sum_{h\in H}|g_0+h\rangle\).

Hint 1 · setup

Write \(\langle y|\mathrm{QFT}_G|g_0+H\rangle\) with both normalizations. Use \(\chi_y(g_0+h)=\chi_y(g_0)\chi_y(h)\).

Hint 2 · key trick

If \(\chi_y(h')\ne1\) for some \(h'\in H\), re-index the sum by \(h\mapsto h+h'\). The sum is unchanged but also multiplied by \(\chi_y(h')\).

Hint 3 · finish

So the sum is \(|H|\) or \(0\). Take \(|\cdot|^2\), then check the probabilities over \(H^\perp\) add to 1 using \(|H^\perp| = |G|/|H|\).

Prefactor in front of \(\sum_{h}\chi_y(h)\)?
Where does the offset \(g_0\) appear in \(\Pr[y]\)?
\[ \langle y|\mathrm{QFT}_G|g_0+H\rangle = \frac{\chi_y(g_0)}{\sqrt{|G||H|}}\sum_{h\in H}\chi_y(h) = \begin{cases}\chi_y(g_0)\sqrt{|H|/|G|} & y\in H^\perp\\ 0 & \text{otherwise}\end{cases} \]

So \(\Pr[y] = |H|/|G|\) on \(H^\perp\), uniform, independent of \(g_0\). Total \(|H^\perp|\cdot|H|/|G| = 1\). Measuring the output register first is optional: every coset gives the same distribution (no-signaling).

Why \(O(\log|G|)\) samples are enough

L13

Let \(K\) be the subgroup generated by the samples so far. Show that while \(K\ne H^\perp\), each new sample has probability at least 1/2 of growing \(K\), and that \(K\) can grow at most \(\log_2|G|\) times.

Hint 1

\(K\) is a proper subgroup of \(H^\perp\). What does Lagrange's theorem say about \(|K|\)?

Hint 2

A uniform sample from \(H^\perp\) lands in \(K\) with probability \(|K|/|H^\perp|\le 1/2\).

Hint 3

When a sample lands outside \(K\), the new generated subgroup strictly contains \(K\), so its size is a multiple of \(|K|\) bigger than \(|K|\).

Do \(\log_2|G|\) samples always generate \(H^\perp\)?

\(|K|\) divides \(|H^\perp|\) and \(K\ne H^\perp\), so \(|K|\le|H^\perp|/2\). Each sample escapes \(K\) with probability \(\ge 1/2\), and each escape at least doubles \(|K|\). Since \(|H^\perp|\le|G|\), at most \(\log_2|G|\) escapes are needed; with \(O(\log|G|+\log(1/\delta))\) samples all of them happen with probability \(\ge 1-\delta\).

Contrast: cyclic \(\mathbb{Z}_N\) usually needs only 2 samples: \(H^\perp\) is cyclic, and \(y_i=c_i N/p\) with \(\gcd(c_1,c_2)=1\) with probability at least \(6/\pi^2\), in which case \(\gcd(y_1,y_2,N)=N/p\). Simon needs about \(n\) because \(H^\perp\) has rank \(n-1\).

Phase kickback, and when it entangles

L7 · L12

Apply controlled-\(U\) to \(|+\rangle|\psi\rangle\) where \(U|\psi\rangle=e^{i\theta}|\psi\rangle\). Then redo it with \(|\phi\rangle=\alpha|\psi_0\rangle+\beta|\psi_1\rangle\), two eigenvectors with different phases.

Hint 1

Expand by linearity and pull the eigenvalue out as a scalar.

With the superposition input, is the result a product state?

Eigenvector: \(\tfrac{1}{\sqrt2}(|0\rangle+e^{i\theta}|1\rangle)\otimes|\psi\rangle\), unentangled. Superposition: \(\tfrac{1}{\sqrt2}\sum_k c_k(|0\rangle+e^{i\theta_k}|1\rangle)|\psi_k\rangle\), entangled when the \(\theta_k\) differ. This is exactly why Shor's input \(|1\rangle\) gives a random \(j/r\).

Where \(8/\pi^2\) comes from

L12

Worst case \(\gamma=y_0+\tfrac12\). Get the limiting value of \(P(y_0)+P(y_1)\) from an integral, then state the exact finite-\(Q\) value.

Hint 1

Hint 2

For the exact value, sum the geometric series: \(\big|\sum_{x=0}^{Q-1}e^{i\phi x}\big| = \big|\sin(Q\phi/2)/\sin(\phi/2)\big|\).

\[ \int_0^1 e^{i\pi t}dt=\frac{2i}{\pi},\qquad \Big|\tfrac{2i}{\pi}\Big|^2=\frac4{\pi^2},\qquad P(y_0)+P(y_1)=\frac{2}{Q^2\sin^2\!\big(\frac{\pi}{2Q}\big)}\ \ge\ \frac8{\pi^2}. \]

From \(\mathrm{QFT}_{\mathbb{Z}_8}|x\rangle=\frac1{\sqrt8}(|0\rangle+\omega^{4x}|1\rangle)(|0\rangle+\omega^{2x}|1\rangle)(|0\rangle+\omega^{x}|1\rangle)\), \(\omega=e^{2\pi i/8}\), derive the gates on each wire. Then say which gates disappear for \(\mathbb{Z}_2^3\) and why.

Does the circuit \(\mathsf{H}, CR_2, CR_3 \,/\, \mathsf{H}, CR_2 \,/\, \mathsf{H}\) output the factors in the order written?

Inversion about the mean, by hand

L14

\(N=4\), the marked item is \(x=2\). Start from \(|s\rangle\), apply the oracle, then \(D=2|s\rangle\langle s|-I\). Show that \(D\) maps each amplitude \(\alpha_x\) to \(2\mu-\alpha_x\), where \(\mu\) is the mean amplitude, and compute the final state.

Hint 1

\(\langle s|\alpha\rangle=\tfrac{1}{\sqrt N}\sum_x\alpha_x=\sqrt N\,\mu\), and every entry of \(|s\rangle\) is \(1/\sqrt N\).

Mean amplitude right after the oracle?

\((2|s\rangle\langle s|\alpha\rangle)_x = 2\cdot\tfrac{1}{\sqrt N}\cdot\sqrt N\mu = 2\mu\), so \((D\alpha)_x=2\mu-\alpha_x\). After \(O\): \((\tfrac12,\tfrac12,-\tfrac12,\tfrac12)\), \(\mu=\tfrac14\). After \(D\): \((0,0,1,0)\). The marked amplitude was far below the mean, so it gets flipped far above it.

How many iterations, and what if you overshoot?

L14

\(N=2^{20}\), \(M=1\). Estimate \(k_{\text{opt}}\). What is the success probability after \(2k_{\text{opt}}\) iterations?

Hint 1

For small \(\theta\), \(\theta\approx\sqrt{M/N}\). You want \((2k+1)\theta\approx\pi/2\).

\(\theta\approx2^{-10}\), so \(k_{\text{opt}}\approx\tfrac{\pi}{4}\cdot2^{10}\approx804\), giving \(P\approx1-O(1/N)\). After \(2k_{\text{opt}}\) steps the angle is within a few \(\theta\) of \(\pi\), so \(P=O(M/N)\) (here about \(9\times10^{-11}\)): Grover is a rotation, and running it longer overshoots. When \(M\) is unknown, pick \(k\) at random from growing ranges (Boyer–Brassard–Høyer–Tapp).

Eigenvalues of the Grover iterate

Restricted to the plane spanned by \(|w\rangle,|r\rangle\), write \(G\) as a \(2\times2\) matrix, find its eigenvalues and eigenvectors, and write \(|s\rangle\) in the eigenbasis.

Hint 1

A rotation by angle \(\phi\) has eigenvalues \(e^{\pm i\phi}\) with eigenvectors \(\tfrac{1}{\sqrt2}(1,\pm i)\) in the basis where it is \(\begin{pmatrix}\cos\phi&\sin\phi\\-\sin\phi&\cos\phi\end{pmatrix}\).

Phase estimation on \(G\) with input \(|s\rangle\) returns

In the basis \((|w\rangle,|r\rangle)\), \(G=\begin{pmatrix}\cos2\theta&\sin2\theta\\-\sin2\theta&\cos2\theta\end{pmatrix}\), which rotates \(|r\rangle\) toward \(|w\rangle\). Eigenvalues \(e^{\pm2i\theta}\), eigenvectors \(|\psi_\pm\rangle=\tfrac{1}{\sqrt2}(|w\rangle\pm i|r\rangle)\). Then \(|s\rangle=\sin\theta|w\rangle+\cos\theta|r\rangle=\tfrac{1}{\sqrt2}\big(-i e^{i\theta}|\psi_+\rangle+i e^{-i\theta}|\psi_-\rangle\big)\): equal magnitude \(\tfrac{1}{\sqrt2}\) on each.

Amplitude estimation vs sampling

L14

You want \(a\) to additive error \(\varepsilon=10^{-3}\). Roughly how many samples does classical estimation need, and how many applications of \(G\) does amplitude estimation need?

Hint 1

Classical: the standard deviation of a sample mean is \(\sqrt{a(1-a)/T}\). Quantum: phase estimation with \(t\) uses resolves the phase to about \(1/t\).

Classical \(T=O(1/\varepsilon^2)\approx10^6\). Quantum \(t=O(1/\varepsilon)\approx10^3\): with a register of size \(Q=t+1\), \(|\tilde a-a|\le\frac{2\pi\sqrt{a(1-a)}}{Q}+\frac{\pi^2}{Q^2}\) with probability at least \(8/\pi^2\) (Brassard–Høyer–Mosca–Tapp). These counts assume \(a\) is bounded away from \(0\) and \(1\). A quadratic speedup, the same one as Grover's.

Abelian HSP · lead frame

1. A random shift becomes an invisible phase

Measuring the output register leaves a random coset \(|g_0+H\rangle\). The QFT turns the shift by \(g_0\) into the phase \(\chi_y(g_0)\), and \(|\chi_y(g_0)|^2=1\). So the randomness you cannot control has no effect on what you measure.

\[ \mathrm{QFT}_G\,|g_0+H\rangle = \sum_{y} \chi_y(g_0)\,\Big(\tfrac{1}{\sqrt{|G||H|}}\textstyle\sum_{h\in H}\chi_y(h)\Big)\,|y\rangle \]

Watch: O'Donnell 1:08–1:11 (coset state). Play: HSP simulator, drag the offset: needles spin, circle sizes stay put. Whiteboard: write \(\mathrm{QFT}_G|g_0+H\rangle\) and factor out \(\chi_y(g_0)\) in one line.

Hides: why the surviving \(y\) are exactly \(H^\perp\). Frame 3 answers that.

Abelian HSP

2. f paints each coset one color

The promise "constant on cosets, distinct across cosets" means \(f\) colors \(G\) in blocks: every coset gets its own color. Measuring \(f\) picks a color, which picks a coset. Equivalently \(f(x)=f(y)\iff x-y\in H\).

Watch: O'Donnell 0:10–0:13 (\(\mathbb{Z}_{24}\), \(H=\langle4\rangle\)) and 0:57–1:02. Whiteboard: in \(\mathbb{Z}_{24}\) with \(H=\langle 4\rangle\), list the four cosets and color them. Then find \(H^\perp\) and check \(|H|\cdot|H^\perp|=24\).

Check your board

\(H=\{0,4,8,12,16,20\}\); cosets \(H,\ 1+H,\ 2+H,\ 3+H\). \(H^\perp=\{y: 4y\equiv0 \pmod{24}\}=\{0,6,12,18\}\). \(6\cdot4=24\) ✓.

Abelian HSP

3. Wrong outcomes close into polygons

The amplitude on \(y\) is a sum of \(|H|\) unit arrows \(\chi_y(h)\). If \(\chi_y\) is trivial on \(H\), they all point the same way. If not, multiplying by \(\chi_y(h')\ne1\) rotates the whole picture onto itself, so the arrows must close into a polygon and sum to zero.

Play: HSP simulator, \(\mathbb{Z}_{12}\), \(H=\langle3\rangle\): tap \(y=1\) (a square) and \(y=4\) (a straight line). Whiteboard: draw the four arrows for \(y=1,2,3,4\) by hand, then prove the "rotates onto itself" argument in two lines.

Abelian HSP

4. A comb transforms to the dual comb

A subgroup of \(\mathbb{Z}_N\) is a comb of spacing \(p\); its Fourier transform is a comb of spacing \(N/p\). Big \(H\) means small \(H^\perp\): \(|H|\cdot|H^\perp|=|G|\). This is L10 §10.2 in group language.

Play: HSP simulator, \(\mathbb{Z}_{12}\), choose "period 5" to see what breaks when \(p\nmid N\) (the real Shor situation). Whiteboard: sketch \(H\) and \(H^\perp\) for \(\mathbb{Z}_{12}\) with \(p=2,3,4,6\) in one table.

Hides: Simon's \(\{0,s\}\) has no "spacing" on a line; there the dual is bitwise orthogonality \(y\cdot s=0\).

Abelian HSP

Every measurement gives one random \(y\in H^\perp\), i.e. one equation that \(H\) must satisfy. Simon: \(y\cdot s\equiv0\). Period finding: a random multiple of \(N/p\), finish with a gcd. Discrete log: \(y_2\equiv s\,y_1 \pmod r\). Enough clues pin down \(H\).

Abelian HSP · the hinge to non-abelian

6. Characters are the eigenbasis of shifts

Let \(T_g|x\rangle=|x+g\rangle\). The character states \(|\hat y\rangle=\frac{1}{\sqrt{|G|}}\sum_x\chi_y(x)|x\rangle\) are simultaneous eigenvectors of every shift:

\[ T_g|\hat y\rangle = \overline{\chi_y(g)}\,|\hat y\rangle . \]

The coset state \(|H\rangle\) is unchanged by every \(T_h\), \(h\in H\), so it can only have weight on \(|\hat y\rangle\) with \(\chi_y(h)=1\) for all \(h\in H\): that is \(H^\perp\). Measuring in this basis is what QFT-then-measure does (up to relabeling \(y\mapsto -y\)). This is why the QFT is the right basis, not a trick.

Whiteboard: verify \(T_g|\hat y\rangle=\overline{\chi_y(g)}|\hat y\rangle\) by substituting \(x'=x+g\). Then say out loud what goes wrong when shifts do not commute. Read: Childs, UMD lecture notes.

Non-abelian HSP

7. The information is there; we cannot read it out

Skip the QFT and think statistics: each query produces a copy of the mixed state \(\rho_H\), and you must tell which of the possible subgroups it came from. There are at most \(|G|^{\log_2|G|}\) subgroups (each has at most \(\log_2|G|\) generators), so polynomially many copies distinguish them in principle. Ettinger, Høyer and Knill (2004) prove \(\mathrm{poly}(\log|G|)\) queries suffice, but their measurement takes exponential time.

Watch: O'Donnell 1:17–1:23 (hypothesis testing). His counting gives about \(\log^2|G|\) copies; the paper's abstract only claims "polynomial in \(\log|G|\)". Unresolved: Gemini claims the bound is \(O(\log|G|)\); I have not confirmed the exponent.

Non-abelian HSP

8. Phases become matrices

In frame 1 the shift became a scalar \(\chi_y(g_0)\) that \(|\cdot|^2\) erased. For non-abelian \(G\), the Fourier transform is built from irreducible representations \(\rho\) that are \(d_\rho\times d_\rho\) matrices, and the shift becomes the matrix \(\rho(g_0)\). Measuring only the label \(\rho\) ("weak Fourier sampling") cannot tell the \(N\) reflection subgroups of \(D_N\) apart at all.

Watch: O'Donnell 0:44–0:46 (what \(D_N\) is) and 1:05–1:07. Caution: his picture colors the \(N\) vertices of the cycle; a real coset partition is of the \(2N\) group elements. A \(D_4\) widget is coming.

Non-abelian HSP

9. Kuperberg: combine phase qubits like a sieve

For \(D_N\), strong Fourier sampling yields qubits \(\tfrac{1}{\sqrt2}(|0\rangle+e^{2\pi i k s/N}|1\rangle)\) with a random known \(k\). Pairing two of them with a CNOT and measuring gives label \(k_1\pm k_2\), randomly. Kuperberg repeatedly cancels low bits until \(k=N/2\), which reveals a bit of \(s\). Time \(2^{O(\sqrt{\log N})}\): subexponential, not polynomial.

Read: Childs, Lecture 8; Kuperberg 2003. No good video of this algorithm exists that I could find.

Grover · lead frame

10. Two reflections make a rotation

The whole algorithm lives in a 2D plane. Reflecting about a line and then about another line at angle \(\theta\) to it rotates by \(2\theta\). The oracle reflects about \(|r\rangle\), the diffusion about \(|s\rangle\), and the angle between them is \(\theta=\arcsin\sqrt{M/N}\).

\[ G^k|s\rangle=\sin\big((2k+1)\theta\big)|w\rangle+\cos\big((2k+1)\theta\big)|r\rangle \]

Play: Grover simulator, apply \(O\) then \(D\) separately. Whiteboard: draw the plane, the mirrors and three steps for \(N=16\), \(M=1\).

Grover

11. Inversion about the mean

The same diffusion step, seen on the amplitudes: \(\alpha_x\mapsto2\mu-\alpha_x\). Before the optimum (and for \(M\ll N\)), the oracle pushes the marked amplitude below the mean; flipping about the mean throws it far above. Early on (while \(k\theta\ll1\)) each step adds about \(2/\sqrt N\) to it; in general step \(k\) adds \(\tfrac{2}{\sqrt N}\cos(2k\theta)\), which shrinks to zero near the optimum.

Play: Grover simulator with \(n\le6\): the dashed line is the mean. Whiteboard: the \(N=4\) drill.

12. Don't overcook it

Success probability \(\sin^2((2k+1)\theta)\) oscillates. Stop at \(k\approx\frac{\pi}{4\theta}-\frac12\), which is \(\approx\frac{\pi}{4}\sqrt{N/M}\) when \(M\ll N\). Twice that brings the success probability back down to \(O(M/N)\), near zero when \(M\ll N\). Unknown \(M\): choose \(k\) at random from growing ranges.

Grover

13. Why \(\sqrt N\) is the best possible

Bennett, Bernstein, Brassard and Vazirani (1997): any quantum algorithm for unstructured search needs \(\Omega(\sqrt N)\) queries. A single query's effect is spread over all \(N\) items, so for a worst-case marked item it changes the state by only \(O(1/\sqrt N)\) compared with an empty oracle (hybrid argument). So Grover gives a quadratic speedup, not an exponential one, and does not by itself make NP-complete problems easy.

Amplitude amplification

14. Grover works on any algorithm, not just \(\mathsf{H}^{\otimes n}\)

If a measurement-free (unitary) algorithm \(A\) succeeds with probability \(a\), replace \(\mathsf{H}^{\otimes n}\) by \(A\) and \(|s\rangle\) by \(A|0\rangle\): the iterate \(Q=-A S_0 A^{-1} S_\chi\) boosts success to near \(1\) (for small \(a\); exact tuning needs a phase or ancilla trick) in \(O(1/\sqrt a)\) rounds, instead of \(O(1/a)\) classical repetitions. Here \(S_\chi\) flips the sign of good states and \(S_0\) flips the sign of \(|0\rangle\).

Amplitude estimation · links L12 to L14

15. Amplitude estimation is phase estimation of \(G\)

On the plane spanned by \(|w\rangle,|r\rangle\), \(G\) is a rotation by \(2\theta\), so its eigenvalues there are \(e^{\pm2i\theta}\). Run L12's phase estimation on \(G\) with input \(|s\rangle\): you learn \(\theta\), hence \(a=\sin^2\theta\), to additive error \(\varepsilon=O(1/t)\) with probability \(\ge8/\pi^2\), using \(t\) uses of \(G\). Classical sampling needs \(O(1/\varepsilon^2)\) samples. Same quadratic speedup, now for estimation.
